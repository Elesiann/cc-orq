## orq tools

These tools start, read and finish orq jobs, the background runs of Codex, OpenCode, Antigravity and Claude CLIs. Every job has a `taskId`, the job id.

- Call `capabilities` before you name a provider instance or a model. It lists each configured instance, its live models, each model's options and allowed values, and its constraints. Do not guess model ids. Its `runtimeMode` and `interactionMode` are the most a task in its own worktree may run as: the lower of the config's `ceiling` and the session's permission mode, which counts as far as the cc-orq hook supplies it; without the hook only the config's `ceiling` holds. `ceiling` in the answer shows each limit and the lower ceiling of a `shared` task.
- `delegate` starts a job and answers `{"taskId", "status": "running"}` at once. It takes `target` (an instance id), `model`, `cwd` (absolute, inside the session directory or an allowed root) and `prompt`, and optionally `effort`, `isolation`, `role`, `runtime` and `interaction`. The worker has no history, so the prompt carries the whole brief, at most 200,000 characters: the goal, the files it may touch and the check that shows it worked. `isolation` is `worktree` by default, a new branch in its own git worktree: tell the worker to commit, because `integrate` merges commits only. Use `shared` only for a task that edits nothing. Leave `runtime` and `interaction` out to get the ceiling. A request above it is refused with `runtime_mode_escalation_denied` or `interaction_mode_escalation_denied`, and the refusal says what would lift it: ask for less, or tell the user, and do not retry other modes to find one that passes. `questions` stays `parent`, since `human` is refused. The answer carries no wake-up, so read the job with `status` when you are ready for it, after other work, and not in a tight loop.
- `status` takes `{"taskId": "<id>"}` and answers one job's state: `running`, `waiting`, `completed`, `failed`, `cancelled` or `interrupted`. Once the job has ended, it carries the `result` or the `error`. It works for jobs of earlier sessions too. A job that stopped on a question has `BLOCKED on a question` as its result: settle the question yourself when the brief and the conversation do, ask the user when they do not, then `delegate` again with the brief and the answer.
- `list` shows the newest jobs first, whoever started them. Pass `{"limit": N}` for more or fewer than 20, up to 50. Use it to find the `taskId` of an older job, then read that job with `status`.
- `cancel` takes `{"taskId": "<id>"}` and an optional `reason`. It stops a running or waiting job, which then reads `cancelled`. A job that has ended answers `task_not_cancellable`.
- `integrate` with `{"taskId": "<id>"}` merges a finished worktree job's branch into the checkout the job was launched from. It merges commits only, so the worker must have committed. When it answers `not_fast_forward`, call it again with `"strategy": "merge"`. When it answers `merge_conflict`, give a rebase of the job's branch to the worker that wrote it, then integrate again. The other refusals say what to fix.
